Why it’s worth checking before your deal ends

  • Avoid the standard variable rate. When a fixed or tracker deal ends, most lenders move you onto their standard variable rate, which is usually higher.
  • Your situation may have changed. If your home has gone up in value or you’ve paid down your mortgage, you may now qualify for better rates.
  • It’s a chance to review everything. Your term, overpayments, borrowing for home improvements, or how your mortgage fits your plans for the next few years.

Stay with your lender, or switch?

You usually have two options when a deal ends. Neither is automatically better. The right choice depends on your circumstances.

Stay with your current lender (a “product transfer”)

  • Often quick, with little paperwork
  • Usually no new credit check or valuation
  • But you only see one lender’s rates

Switch to a new lender (a “remortgage”)

  • Compare rates from a wide range of lenders
  • A chance to borrow more or change your term
  • Involves a new application and checks

We can advise on both, so you get an honest comparison rather than a sales pitch. If staying with your lender is the better option, we’ll tell you.

What we’ll look at with you

  • What your current lender is offering you
  • Whether other lenders could offer a better overall deal (including fees, not just the rate)
  • Any early repayment charges, and when they end
  • Whether you want to borrow more, for example for home improvements
  • The right length of fix (or tracker) for your plans
  • Whether your mortgage term still suits you

Could overpaying save you money?

If you have spare cash, overpaying can reduce the interest you pay and shorten your mortgage. Check your lender’s overpayment allowance first to avoid charges.
