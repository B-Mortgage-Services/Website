How buy-to-let lending works differently

A buy-to-let mortgage is assessed differently from the one on your own home. It’s worth knowing where the differences are before you start looking at properties.

  • Lending is based mainly on the rent. Lenders look at the rent the property is expected to achieve, and apply their own stress test to it, rather than looking only at your personal income.
  • Deposits are usually larger. Lenders typically expect more than they would on a residential mortgage, and the rates available often improve as your deposit grows.
  • Many are interest-only. Monthly payments cover the interest and the balance doesn’t reduce, so you need a plan for repaying the capital at the end of the term.
  • Some lenders want you to own your own home already, or to have a minimum personal income, before they’ll lend on a rental property.
  • Personal name or limited company. Both are common. They’re assessed differently and have different tax consequences.

Who we help

  • First-time landlords. Buying your first rental property, including if you already own the home you live in.
  • Growing a portfolio. Adding properties, and the criteria that change once you hold several.
  • Limited company landlords. Buying through a company, including special purpose vehicles.
  • Remortgaging a rental. Your deal is ending, or you want to release equity to buy another.
  • Moving out, keeping the property. Letting your current home and buying another, sometimes called let-to-buy.
  • Inherited or unexpected landlords. Where letting wasn’t the plan. These can count as consumer buy-to-let, which is regulated.

A word on tax

How rental income and property ownership are taxed depends on your circumstances, and on whether you hold the property personally or through a company. It changes often, and it can make a real difference to whether an investment works.

We advise on the mortgage. We don’t give tax advice, and we’d always suggest speaking to an accountant or tax adviser before deciding how to structure a purchase.
