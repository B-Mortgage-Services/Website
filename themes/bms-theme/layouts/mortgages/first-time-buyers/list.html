Your first mortgage, step by step

Here’s the whole journey at a glance. We’ll be with you for every part of it.

  1. 1 Save your deposit. Usually at least 5% of the property price, and more can get you better rates.
  2. 2 Get an Agreement in Principle. An indication from a lender of what they may lend you. Estate agents often ask for one before accepting an offer. We help here
  3. 3 Find your home. Viewings, and working out what you can comfortably afford.
  4. 4 Offer accepted. Now things start moving.
  5. 5 Full mortgage application. We prepare and submit it, and deal with the lender for you. We help here
  6. 6 Valuation and survey. The lender values the property. You may also want your own survey.
  7. 7 Mortgage offer. The lender formally confirms your mortgage. We help here
  8. 8 Exchange of contracts. The purchase becomes legally binding and your deposit is paid.
  9. 9 Completion: you get the keys. The money is transferred and the home is yours.

From application to completion, we keep things moving, chase the lender and solicitors, and keep you updated, so you always know what’s happening.

How much deposit do I need?

Most lenders ask for a deposit of at least 5% of the property price. A bigger deposit usually means access to lower rates, so it’s worth knowing where the thresholds are, typically at 10%, 15%, 25% and 40%.

Some lenders offer options for buyers with smaller deposits, such as mortgages for people with a strong record of paying rent, or schemes where family members help. These come with stricter criteria, so it’s worth talking them through with us before you rely on one.

Help from family

If parents or family want to help, they might gift you money towards your deposit, or in some cases add their income to your application without going on the deeds (known as “Joint Borrower Sole Proprietor”). Each option has different implications for everyone involved, and we’ll explain them clearly.

Get offer-ready before you start viewing

When you find the right place, being ready to move quickly can make a real difference. An Agreement in Principle (sometimes called a Decision in Principle) shows the estate agent you’re a serious buyer.

  • Photo ID: passport or driving licence
  • Proof of income: your last 3 months’ payslips (or 2 years’ tax calculations if you’re self-employed)
  • Bank statements: last 3 months
  • Proof of deposit: savings statements, or a gift letter if family are helping
